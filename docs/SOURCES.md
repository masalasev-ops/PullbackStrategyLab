# SOURCES.md

Where the strategy this lab models is stated, and what each of the twenty gate clauses rests on.

The lab models the published methodology of **Martin Luk**. Until 2026-09-09 no document in this
corpus named him, named a source, or carried a single quotation, and the gate set was traced on
2026-09-08 against the corpus alone. That trace found six of twenty clause forms resting on a stated
source and fourteen an author's reading, which was a true statement about the corpus and not about
the material. This document is the material, read and quoted, and the same twenty clauses traced
against it.

**This is a spec, not a record.** It is edited cleanly and its prior text goes to `CHANGELOG.md`,
because a check reads it as the current statement of what each clause rests on and a document
corrected by appending would carry two answers. (see: The corpus is nine documents, and the ninth
records where the strategy came from)

**Nothing here changes a threshold, a gate or a timeframe.** Every disagreement named below is
recorded so it can become a decision with a citation. Acting on one is separate work and is due at
the operator, because choosing between the source and the lab is authoring the strategy rather than
implementing it.

## How a clause is traced

Each of the twenty clauses is traced twice, on the two things a gate is made of.

**The form** is what the clause tests. **The threshold** is the number it tests against. They are
recorded separately because they fail differently: a form with no source is a question nobody asked
for, and a threshold with no source is a number somebody chose. The 2026-09-08 trace kept them apart
for that reason and this one keeps the same two columns so the two traces can be compared.

Each verdict is one of four words, and they mean exactly this:

| Verdict | What it means |
|---|---|
| `his own words` | A source of his own words states it, and this document quotes that statement |
| `his own words, in part` | One half of a two-clause form is stated in his own words and the other half is not. The row says which half |
| `no source found` | Nothing in the seven sources states it. Not a claim that he does not do it, only that no source read here says he does |
| `carries no threshold` | The clause compares words rather than a quantity, so there is no number for a source to have stated |

**A clause verdict of `his own words` obliges a quotation in that clause's section below.** Paraphrase
is what produced fourteen author's readings in the first place: a sentence describing what a trader
does is indistinguishable, once written down, from a sentence describing what the writer took him to
mean. So a sourced clause quotes, and a check fails a sourced clause that does not.

**The `Bearing on` column names sources bearing on the clause, which is not the same as sources that
establish it.** A clause verdict of `no source found` may still name sources, because what a source
says about a clause it does not establish is often the useful part: `tradable-shortable` names `P3`
precisely because `P3` shows him doing the opposite.

**The `Disagreement` column means the source states something the lab measures differently**, on a
different chart, from a different level, in a different unit, or as a hard gate where the source says
it is not one. A clause the source is simply silent about is not a disagreement; it is the
`no source found` verdict and nothing more. Some clauses carry a tension worth reading that is not a
disagreement in this sense, and those are in the prose rather than the column.

## The long clauses, quoted

### tradable, long

**What he specifies.** Liquidity measured in dollars rather than shares, twice, and a preference for
the most liquid of a moving group.

> "I use the EMAs and the dollar volumes. So it's not the volume like a simple like the how many
> shares that you trade, is on the dollar amount that the stock is trading."
> `P1`, 25:50

> "I would prioritize trading the one with the highest dollar volume. So it would be more liquid, so
> it would be maybe less slippage."
> `P1`, 3:18:39

On low-priced names he states the opposite of an exclusion:

> "I currently don't face problems with [slippage], except when trading stocks below $5, for which I
> reduce my position size."
> `P3`

**What the lab implements.** At least $20 million median daily dollar volume, and price above $5,
both as hard gates.

**Where they part.** Measuring liquidity in dollars is his and the lab does it, which is why the form
is sourced in part. Neither number is his: no source states $20 million, and the only figures offered
anywhere are a narrator's optional suggestion in `S3` of "market cap above $100 million or cash
volume above, say, $1 million", two orders of magnitude away from the lab's and not attributed to
him. **The $5 floor contradicts him.** He trades below $5 and manages the cost by sizing down; the
lab refuses the name.

### moves-enough, long

**What he specifies.** The figure, and immediately the condition under which he sets it aside.

> "And I try to focus on the fast-moving stocks in ho[t] [sector] which is larger than 5% ADR."
> `P2`, 26:26

> "I mainly trade stocks with >5% ADR for this reason (as illustrated by most trades above), but this
> isn't a hard rule. I sometimes trade slower stocks with a tighter stop, depending on what's working
> lately and the quality of the setup."
> `P3`

**What the lab implements.** Typical daily range of 5% or more, identical on both lists, as the gate
applied before anything else is computed.

**Where they part.** This is the best-sourced number in the lab: form and threshold both, in his own
words, in two independent sources. ARCHITECTURE's basis cell reading "From the trader's stated
preference" is exactly right and can now carry a citation. **What the lab has is the number without
its qualifier.** He names the trade he makes against it, a slower name taken with a tighter stop, and
the lab has no way to express that: a name at 4% range is refused whatever its stop would have been.
This is the clause where a stated source and a faithful implementation come apart, and it is worth
saying plainly because a sourced threshold reads as settled.

### uptrend, long

**What he specifies.** The ladder, written out.

> "Three watchlists categorised by the stock's strength based on its EMAs. Lead: 9 EMA > 21 EMA > 50
> EMA. Mediocre: 9 EMA > 21 EMA < 50 EMA or 9 EMA < 21 EMA > 50 EMA. Lag: 9 EMA < 21 EMA < 50 EMA."
> `P3`

> "I use like three indicators, the EMAs, the 9, 21 and 50, and I also include the 150 too."
> `P1`, 25:50

And slope rather than order alone: `S1` describes him looking for stocks "trading above rising moving
averages", and `S3` that he avoids names "below declining 9 and 21 EMAs while making lower highs and
lower lows".

**What the lab implements.** Price above the 9-day average, which is above the 21-day, which is above
the 50-day. Exponential, seeded on the simple average of the first period, computed once in
`Averages.Exponential`.

**Where they part.** His written ladder is the lab's ladder, period for period, and exponential on
both sides. Three things he specifies that the lab does not test: **the 150 EMA**, named as a fourth
indicator and used as a support in `P1`; **slope**, since his own ladder plus "rising" is strictly
stronger than order alone; and **higher highs and higher lows**. The lab also adds a clause he does
not, requiring price above the 9, where his partition is between the averages only.

### thrust, long

**What he specifies.** Three scans, named and dated.

> "I spend around 30 minutes a day scanning for the previous day's high performers ('potent scan')
> and pre-market gap-ups ('pre-market scan'). On weekends, I also scan for the biggest movers of the
> past 30 days ('leader scan')."
> `P3`

`S3` adds a screen its narrator attributes to him: stocks up at least 30% over 1, 3 or 6 months,
taking "the top 20 to 30% of the stocks from the list".

**What the lab implements.** Appeared on an upward mover scan within the last ten days. Six scans,
three a side: yesterday's biggest movers, gaps from the previous close, and the biggest movers of the
past twenty sessions, each taking the top 50 by its own magnitude.

**Where they part.** The scan set is his, near enough one for one. Three parameters are the lab's:
the ten-session recency window appears in no source; the long-horizon lookback is twenty sessions
against his thirty days; and his leader scan runs weekly where the lab runs all six nightly, so a
name enters the lab's month-mover population up to four days earlier than it would enter his.

### dip-shape, long

**What he specifies.** That a pullback can be too deep, without a number:

> "It's a really deep deep pullback. So I don't think this is a really good entry."
> `P1`, 1:09:25

And the structure it sits inside, which is a base rather than a drift. `S1` gives the consolidation
as lasting "eight weeks or longer", and in `P2` he describes a six-month base on SMCI that "never
violates the weekly 21 EMA". The pullback he buys inside it is a single intraday flush.

**What the lab implements.** Two to seven days of drifting, giving back no more than 40% of the jump,
measured on daily bars from the thrust extreme.

**Where they part.** The depth clause has a form and no threshold: he says a pullback can be too deep
and nobody says 40%. **The duration clause is the substantive disagreement.** No source describes a
two-to-seven-session drift as the thing being bought. What the sources describe is a consolidation of
eight weeks or longer inside which price flushes and recovers within one session. The lab's window is
a different timeframe from the one the pattern is defined on, which is the same mismatch
`exit-tight` carries and which reaches this clause first in the funnel.

### held-floor, long

**What he specifies.** Not that the floor was never lost, but that it was lost and taken back.

> "[This candle] is undercut and reclaim of the 9 EMA."
> `P1`, 1:29:04

`S1` makes it the third of three rules: "Enter On Reclamation. Rather than buying blindly into
weakness, Martin waits for price to reclaim support, buyers to demonstrate control, a strong close or
positive reversal."

**What the lab implements.** No daily close below the 21-day average at any point during the dip.

**Where they part.** That the setup turns on a support level holding is his, which is why the form is
sourced in part. **The lab asks it of a different object and at a different level.** His test is a
reclaim on the entry session, the level having been broken intraday and taken back by the close; the
lab's is an unbroken run of closes through the whole dip, which refuses exactly the session he would
buy. And the level he names is most often the 9 EMA or the anchored average price. Nothing chooses
the 21.

### contraction, long

**What he specifies.** Tightening before the move, repeatedly.

> "It forms two tight candles after failing from the breakouts."
> `P2`, 50:33

> "The 9 and 21, they're really packed."
> `P1`, 1:47:30

`S2` lists "tight inside days" and "clean range expansion" among the four things he waits for, and
`S3` closes its account of the setup on "narrowing range".

**What the lab implements.** The latest day's range is narrower than its own 20-day average range.

**Where they part.** They do not, on the question. The form is his and ARCHITECTURE's basis cell
reading "Authored" understates it. The comparison window is nobody's: no source names twenty days,
and his own instances are tight candles and an inside day, which compare against the immediately
preceding bars rather than against a month. That is an unsourced threshold rather than a
disagreement, and it is recorded as one.

### trigger-near, long

**What he specifies.** A chase filter, with a number.

> "I'm not chasing an entry if the stock is really far away from the low of day or the ideal entry
> point. So if it's up for more than 3% from the low of day then I'm just avoiding it."
> `P1`, 24:22

He offers it as one of the reasons his average loss fell in 2025.

**What the lab implements.** The trigger sits within 1.5 daily ranges of the current price.

**Where they part.** A chase filter is his and the lab has one, so the form is sourced.
**Everything else about it differs.** His reference is the current session's low and the lab's is the
previous close. His unit is per cent and the lab's is daily ranges. His cut is 3% and the lab's 1.5
ranges, which on a name at the 5% range floor is 7.5%. These are not one test with two constants, and
a source now exists for a rule the lab does not have.

### exit-tight, long

**What he specifies.** The cap, three times, across two years and two media.

> "I limit my stops at less than 50% of these stock's average [ADR], so maximum will be 5%. But I
> really don't set my stops that wide currently. I think I rarely would set my stop wider than 3% or
> even 2.5%."
> `P1`, 17:48

> "As I became more experienced, I tightened my stops to 1 to 5% (mostly), and avoided exceeding half
> of the stock's average daily range (ADR), so, no more than a 2.5% stop for a 5% ADR stock. [...]
> Over 80% of my trades in 2024 used stops of <3.5%."
> `P3`

> "Trying to limit my stop at less than 50% of the stock's ADR, the average daily range. So the
> maximum stop I would take is around 5%."
> `P2`, 25:48

**What the lab implements.** Entry to give-up distance no more than half the stock's daily range. One
clause.

**Where they part.** The cap is his, so ARCHITECTURE's attribution to "The strategy's own rule" is
exact and can now be cited. **The lab implements one clause of a three-clause rule.** He also carries
an absolute ceiling of 5% whatever the range, which binds on any name above a 10% range, and a
working maximum of 2.5% to 3% he says he rarely exceeds. Neither exists in the lab. The level the
distance is measured from is separately his and separately not the lab's, and that is set out under
"The stop rule is two-part" below.

### cluster, long

**What he specifies.** Grouping as a reason to attend, and what he then waits for.

> "When several stocks within the same sector newly appear on my scans (so not existing leading
> themes), or 'upgrade' to a stronger watchlist, I pay more attention to them. I monitor those stocks
> closely during the next pullback to look for entries."
> `P3`

And which of a moving group he takes:

> "When there are maybe three or four names that is moving together, I just wanted to take one or two
> of them, and then I am going to buy the faster moving ones."
> `P1`, 18:56

**What the lab implements.** Two or more stocks from the same industry flagged the same night.
Recorded but not required in the baseline.

**Where they part.** They do not. Same-sector grouping, the same night, and explicitly not a gate:
"I pay more attention to them" is what "recorded, never gating in the baseline" implements. The count
is the lab's, since he says "several" and elsewhere "three or four", which is an unsourced threshold
rather than a disagreement. One thing his sentence carries that the lab has nowhere: the grouping is
what makes him wait for the next pullback, so for him the cluster and the pattern are one sequence
where the lab records two independent facts.

## The short clauses, quoted

### tradable-shortable, short

**What he specifies.** Nothing. Across roughly six hours of primary material in which he shorts
throughout and attributes about 30% of a period's profit to it, borrow does not come up once: not
availability, not cost, not a locate, not a filter standing in for any of them.

What he does say points away from the lab's floors. He keeps a watchlist specifically for small caps
"(<$5B market cap)" (`P3`), and shorts small and micro caps in `P1`.

**What the lab implements.** Price above $5, market capitalisation above $2 billion, median dollar
volume above $50 million, and at least 90 sessions since first listing.

**Where they part.** This is the one clause of the twenty where no source is not a defect, and
ARCHITECTURE's own basis cells already say why: these are proxies for information the price feed does
not carry, invented by the lab because a paper record of an unfillable short is fiction. **They are
the lab's engineering rather than the strategy's rule, and they should be recorded as that rather
than as an author's reading of a silent source.** Worth stating separately that they are stricter
than his practice, so the short side's population is narrower than the trader's by construction, and
every short funnel figure is over that narrower population.

### moves-enough, short

**What he specifies.** As on the long list, and stated without reference to direction.

> "And I try to focus on the fast-moving stocks in ho[t] [sector] which is larger than 5% ADR."
> `P2`, 26:26

> "I mainly trade stocks with >5% ADR for this reason (as illustrated by most trades above), but this
> isn't a hard rule."
> `P3`

**What the lab implements.** Identical to the long side.

**Where they part.** The same verdict and the same reason: the number is sourced and the hardness is

### downtrend, short

**What he specifies.** The inverted ladder, written out, and slope in practice.

> "Lag: 9 EMA < 21 EMA < 50 EMA."
> `P3`

> "The price reacts to the 50 EMA and also to the anchored VWAP and they found resistance, and the
> EMAs are trending down."
> `P1`, 1:15:22

**What the lab implements.** Price below the 9-day average, below the 21-day, below the 50-day.

**Where they part.** His "Lag" list is the lab's `downtrend` exactly, and it is his written
definition rather than an inference from his charts. The same two gaps as the long side: the lab adds
a price-below-the-9 clause his ladder does not carry, and tests position where he reads position and
direction of travel together. **On this side the slope clause does more work**, because his stated
ideal is averages actively curling down and crossing, which is a stronger condition than three
numbers in an order.

### averages-squeezing, short

**What he specifies.** The observation ARCHITECTURE attributes to him, found, twice, and both times
on a short.

> "For SMCI, one of the main reasons that I would really want to focus on SMCI is the weekly chart,
> as the weekly chart is really on a downtrend and the key moving averages are really close to each
> other."
> `P2`, 1:50:10

> "The ideal one would be the first breakdown of the 50 EMA and then the first pullback into the 50
> EMA, and generally the 21 or the nine would be really also near the 50 EMA too, and they start
> really curling back and start crossing down the 50 EMA."
> `P1`, 3:43:53

**What the lab implements.** The gap between the 21-day and 50-day averages is narrower than its own
average over the last 20 sessions, on daily bars.

**Where they part.** **This is the one clause whose 2026-09-08 verdict reverses.** That trace moved
this form from stated source to author's reading because nothing in the corpus recorded the
observation. The observation exists, in his own words, and the ARCHITECTURE cell reading "The
trader's stated observation is that the tighter those averages, the more reliably a bounce is
rejected there" was right. **The disagreement is the chart.** His statement is about the weekly
averages converging; the lab computes a daily gap against its own twenty-session history. Those are
different quantities that can point opposite ways in the same week, and nothing in the lab reads a
weekly bar at all.

### thrust, short

**What he specifies.** No downward scan. All three scanners he names are long-side by construction:
previous day's high performers, pre-market gap-ups, and biggest movers of the past 30 days, the last
of which `P3` states without naming a direction.

He describes reaching shorts by inverting the long structure instead:

> "I just flip the short side around and then it will be the long side, and they are really similar."
> `P1`, 15:14

**What the lab implements.** Appeared on a downward mover scan within the last ten days, from three
short scans mirroring the three long ones.

**Where they part.** They do not disagree, because he states nothing to disagree with. The 2026-09-08
verdict of an author's reading stands and now has a reason: the mirror is the lab's. His short
candidates come from the same long-biased scan set plus a watchlist he already keeps, which is a
materially different population from names ranked by how far they fell. **The short side's entry
population is the lab's invention and is recorded here as one.**

### bounce-shape, short

**What he specifies.** Nothing about the shape of the bounce. What he specifies is where it must
reach, which is `reached-ceiling`: he shorts the rally that arrives at declining averages or an
anchored level and is rejected there. The nearest figure is a holding period rather than a bounce
length, being shorts held "for only maybe two to three days" (`P1`, 25:18).

**What the lab implements.** Two to seven days of rising, recovering no more than 40% of the drop.

**Where they part.** They do not, for want of anything to part over. Neither number nor form appears
in any source. **A tension worth recording that is not a disagreement:** this clause and
`reached-ceiling` can pull against each other, because that clause asks whether the bounce arrived at
the level where it is expected to stall and this one refuses a bounce that recovered more than 40% of
the drop. On a name whose declining 21 or 50 sits further than 40% back up the move, no bounce
satisfies both. Whether the two ever bind against each other on the same row is measurable over the
calibration store and is not measured here.

### reached-ceiling, short

**What he specifies.** An anchored level and declining averages acting as a ceiling.

> "It overshoots the 21 EMA and then touches the anchored VWAP, just gets rejected and then goes
> straight down. So this is a really good clean [entry] on both the declining 21 EMA and also the
> anchored VWAP, and they both when they really came close to each other."
> `P1`, 1:27:42

And that the levels must coincide rather than any one of them serving:

> "When those support levels really coincide or really come close to each other, those areas is where
> you should put your focus."
> `P1`, 1:11:21

And, on the anchor, that he selects it by what the chart has already done:

> "Sometimes I'm going to adjust it a little bit to find the best candle to which the price would
> respect the most. [...] I'm trying to match the anchored VWAP to the most number of reaction points
> possible."
> `P1`, 1:19:46 and 1:43:37

**What the lab implements.** Price within half a daily range of the 21-day or 50-day average, or of
the declining average price anchored to the last swing high, the anchor fixed at the swing the thrust
ran from (see: The anchored average price is anchored at the swing the thrust ran from).

**Where they part, on two counts, and the lab is right about one of them.** **The first is a
